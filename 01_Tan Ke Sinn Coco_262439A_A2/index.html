Things You Should Do

Align Text To Left

text is typically aligned to the left side so as to improve readability, since there are less text rags (uneven ends) than if your text were to be aligned to the centre. additionally, since most people read left to right, it is generally more recommended to align text to the left, rather than the right.

Keep Your Headings Short

Headings should generally be around seven words, and span across one line at best. Headings are the big idea that you aim to present, a quick conclusion of that specific point. You want it short. You want it simple. That’s how you can efficiently capture your audience’s attention.

Avoid Cluttering Of Visuals

Sometimes you have a lot of ideas and a lot of visuals that compliment, but what happens when you have far too many of them? Suddenly, the main focus of your slide is driven away by the colourful charts that you present to them. That’s not what you want. What you want is a clean. simplistic feel to your slides. You want an adequate use of visuals, kept to three or less, that guide your audience to the big idea, instead of distracting them away from it.

Use Contrasting Colours

Keep a high contrast between the text and background colours, it enhances the clarity of your content, helping the audience read it better. If you find yourself struggling to match background and text colours, one easy way is to use a very dark background against a much lighter text colour. Things You Shouldn't Do

Don't Insert Walls Of Text

Do you ever stare at a large wall of text and find yourself lost two sentences in? You hear the presenter reading, but the words seem to fade into background noise? Sometimes, more is less. You may want to convey a lot of ideas that come off as wordy, but you’ll soon realise that your audience has already derailed from your train of thoughts!

  • Instead, you should cut down on the words shown on a slide, and only leave in the key points. If all the important points dont fit in one slide, how about we try breaking them down into multiple?

Don't Use "Ugly" Fonts

Cursive and calligraphy fonts, while classy, are generally hard to read, especially for people with reading disabilities. This makes it harder for them to understand the point that you are conveying!

While undertale characters are generally loved, please dont use the fonts they were named after — "comic sans" and "papyrus". The lack of a consistent visual weight makes your text appear informal, which can heavily impact the tone that you are trying to set for your presentation

  • Instead, try opting for the traditional san serif fonts, like arial

Don't Overuse Capital Letters

"LOOK OVER HERE! THIS IS THE DESCRIPTION OF AN IMPORTANT POINT THAT I WANT TO SHARE, AND I WILL PUT ALL THE TEXT IN CAPITAL LETTERS SO YOU SEE THEM CLEARLY!!!" Does it feel a little bit overwhelming? The use of all caps in text makes you look like youre yelling at your audience, and there is no longer a distinct emphasis on the important part of your point – which means your important points are no longer important!

  • Ideally, if you wanted to use capital letters, stick to using them only in headers. That’s your safest option.

Dont Overuse Transitions

Sometimes you find your slide folding itself into a paper airplane and flying away to unveil the next slide. That’s not necessarily wrong, but sometimes it feels distracting, and can put a total offset to the mood of your presentation. Some animations, when excessively used, may lead your audience to think of thise slide deck as poorly designed, such that it feels unprofessional!

  • What you can do, however, is to limit the use of transitions; use them only when you feel like it is related to the theme of your slides, or help to control their pacing
